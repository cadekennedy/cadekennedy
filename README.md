## About Me
As a recent graduate from Tennessee Technological University, I hold both a Master's and Bachelor's degree in Computer Science. My undergraduate studies focused on Data Science & Machine Learning, with a minor in Mathematics.

Before completing my master's degree, I joined EPB in Chattanooga, TN, as a Quantum Software Engineering Fellow. This fellowship was a 1-Year NIST funded research program (ending in October 2026) with a primary focus on the development of Hybrid Quantum-Classical algorithms. In this role, I gained hands-on experience in a variety of areas, including:

- Developing optimization methods for optimal power flow
- Compiling and transpiling quantum circuits
- QAOA Formulation for cost function encoding
- Exploring quantum advantage over classical computation
- Building and integrating CI pipelines into a web application
- Connecting to a persistent database
  
This fellowship deepened my understanding of the daily software development lifecycle. I am now ready to find a role that not only aligns with my interests but, more importantly, challenges me to continuously learn and develop new skills.

## Professional Experience

### Quantum Software Engineering Fellow — *EPB, Chattanooga, TN* `Oct 2025 – Oct 2026`

- Earned certification through IonQ & Classiq's **CQ Accelerate** quantum computing program, covering quantum math, algorithms, and live QPU hardware execution.
- Partnered with **Oak Ridge National Lab, NVIDIA, and IonQ** to adapt Distribution Network Reconfiguration into a quantum-classical hybrid optimization workflow, achieving feasibility on up to **14 qubits (QPU)** and **28 qubits (simulation)**.
- Built a classical optimization framework using **Genetic Algorithms and Graph Neural Networks** to minimize electric grid line losses via AC Power Flow — achieving the known optimal solution on the **IEEE 33-bus benchmark** (a **31% reduction** from the base configuration).
- Investigated quantum advantage for grid optimization by leveraging **QAOA** to explore larger search spaces and warm-start a classical solver; convergence results actively being benchmarked.
